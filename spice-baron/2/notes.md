# 1.1 - Yoruban Emissary
- Kehinde leads the Yoruba delegation — arrives on horseback, dressed in ceremonial armor
- Aesthetics TBD: post-collapse world 100 years out; Yoruba recovered to stable state within a decade and is likely more technologically advanced than the Plantation; their armor/dress may blend pre-collapse materials with new craft
- This is a **routine annual visit** — trade contract renegotiation for the year; the Baron has a practiced, polished face for it; the Plantation's interior reality is not advertised
- The delegation stays several days; the Baron wines and dines them; hospitality as performance and competition
- **The Baron's ulterior motive**: not just to trade but to gain influence and control inside Yoruba — political leverage to protect the Plantation long-term; he is trying to get hooks into them while appearing to be the gracious host
- Táiwò's identity as a famous chef in Yoruba is public knowledge — he is not undercover; his reputation is the reason the Baron wanted him
- Kehinde and Táiwò's sibling relationship is not a secret; she teases him, is openly warm with him; they are visibly close
- Gardner witnesses this — the first time he has seen Táiwò in relation to someone who knew him before the Plantation; it is disorienting in a way Gardner cannot yet name; Táiwò has a whole world Gardner has no access to
- **Táiwò pulls Gardner in as assistant chef for the full visit**: the delegation is an opportunity to demonstrate what the plantation's ingredients can actually do in skilled hands; Gardner is present throughout, learning, watching, working alongside Táiwò for days in close quarters
- The visit is a busy, high-stakes stretch — the Baron's eyes are on the food and the diplomacy; Gardner and Táiwò's growing closeness happens in the margins, in the shared labor of preparation and service; it is unremarkable to anyone watching
