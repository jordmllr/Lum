# Spice Baron Voice and Style Guide

## Voice summary

Spice Baron should read like southern gothic science fiction seen from inside a hothouse: lush, fragrant, hierarchical, and cruel. The prose stays close to Gardner and translates power through the body. Hunger, shame, desire, scent, heat, labor, and terror should all feel immediate. Beauty is never neutral in this book. It is cultivated, hoarded, weaponized, sold, or mourned.

The baseline voice is literary and sensuous, but disciplined. The novel earns its lushness through precision. When scenes turn violent, the syntax should tighten and the images should become cleaner, harder, and more ceremonial.

## Core prose rules

1. Stay close to Gardner. Describe the world through what he notices, fears, wants, and has learned to endure.
2. Use concrete sensory detail before abstract interpretation. Let readers smell the cinnamon, feel the damp heat, see the bruising on fruit and skin.
3. Build the world through ritual, labor, trade, and material detail rather than explanation.
4. Make ownership a constant pressure in the language. The Baron does not simply command; he appraises, keeps, displays, breeds, and uses.
5. Let tenderness and horror share space. The romance should sharpen the brutality, not soften it.
6. Keep the prose most expansive in greenhouses, kitchens, conservatories, and hallucinatory states. Elsewhere, remain taut.
7. Write violence as deliberate spectacle. Avoid action-movie pace. The point is dread, ceremony, and violation.

## Lexical preferences

Favor words drawn from these semantic fields:

- Botany: bloom, graft, peel, root, husk, stem, vine, rind, sap, pulp, rot, loam, conservatory, hothouse
- Cuisine: steep, pare, char, distill, brine, rind, clove, bark, syrup, broth, smoke, sweetness, bitter
- Possession and hierarchy: keep, stock, ledger, inheritance, breeding, steward, tribute, inventory, spoil, estate
- Body and ritual: throat, wrist, scar, marrow, breath, pulse, posture, procession, offering, witness

Use technical or elevated words when they clarify texture. Avoid sounding thesaurus-driven.

## Syntax and rhythm

- Default to medium-length sentences with clean control.
- Expand into longer, accumulating sentences when Gardner is overwhelmed by beauty, lust, memory, or altered perception.
- Break into shorter sentences at moments of violence, realization, or humiliation.
- Avoid constant fragments. Save them for genuine shock or disgust.

## Imagery rules

- The primary image systems are botanical and culinary.
- Pair beauty with cost. A greenhouse should suggest sweat, maintenance, scarcity, and extraction, not just wonder.
- Let recurring contrasts organize the book: hot/cold, wet/dry, cultivated/wild, feast/famine, perfume/blood, preservation/decay.
- Yoruba should feel expansive, civic, and living, not merely exotic or luxurious. Its beauty comes from stewardship and shared culture rather than the Baron's private hoard.

## Character voice notes

### Gardner
His interiority is observant, restrained, and body-conscious. He notices work, tools, materials, posture, and risk. He should not sound academically reflective. When he speaks at length, it should feel earned by pressure, revelation, or grief.

### The Baron
He speaks with proprietary calm. He should sound civilized in a way that makes the violence worse. He treats people, spices, and architecture as proof of taste and dominion.

### The Chef
The Chef should sound exact, sensuous, and cultivated. He understands ingredients as structure and possibility, not mere indulgence. His language can open Gardner's world without becoming a sermon.

## Scene-by-scene tonal targets

### Plantation and courtyard
Dry menace, ritualized hierarchy, the beauty of wealth gone rancid. Keep the prose spare enough that the cruelty lands cleanly.

### Greenhouses and kitchens
Most lush register. Heat, fragrance, condensation, bruised leaves, bark, sugar, knives, steam. This is where sensuality and threat most naturally braid together.

### Yoruba
Open the sentence structure slightly. Let the prose breathe. Use more civic, architectural, and communal detail to contrast with the Baron's enclosed world.

### Final revenge and blooming
Ceremonial horror. The language may become more visionary, but the physical sequence must remain easy to follow. Readers should always know what Gardner is doing and why.

## Hard avoids

- Do not slip into generic bleak dystopia.
- Do not write the Baron as a witty modern sadist.
- Do not make Gardner passive in the prose even when he is trapped; his attention is his form of agency.
- Do not romanticize the plantation aesthetic without showing the violence that sustains it.
- Do not let lyrical passages lose spatial clarity.
- Do not flatten Yoruba into a utopia with no political texture.

## Sample paragraph

The greenhouse took him in like a fever. Wet heat silvered the inside of the glass and turned every row of spice trees half spectral, bark dark with condensation, leaves slick as tongues. Cinnamon peel lay curling in shallow baskets at his feet, red-brown and fragrant, and the smell of it was almost enough to hide the iron stink still clinging to the back of his throat from the courtyard. Gardner stood very still among the ordered abundance, among the trees the Baron kept trimmed and numbered and named as if naming were the same thing as love, and listened for the scrape of boots on the flagstones behind him.
