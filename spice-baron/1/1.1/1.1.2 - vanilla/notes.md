# Scene 2
we've just come off of the first scene, where the Baron has grotesquely executed someone. So far, it is unclear what Gardner's relationship to the Baron is. This scene is going to establish their closeness. The Baron pays special attention to Gardner. 

1.1.2.1 - Gardner makes his way to the greenhouse
we get some more sense of the setting and layout of the plantation. We also get some of Gardners internal thoughts as he tries to leave behind the reckoning.

1.1.2.2 - Gardner gathers the pollination supplies and begins to lose himself in his work

1.1.2.3 - Baron monologues and teases Gardner
Baron comes into the greenhouse and asks him about the reckoning. Gardner is quiet and tries to be small and acquiesce. The Baron taunts him for being such a fragile flower. All this time he is caressing the orchids talking about the irrational power of beauty. How even a starving man will seek it out, even if it is a waste of time or energy. 

1.1.2.4 - Baron crushes the vanilla orchid - possessing beauty
Brings home his philosophy that beauty is a weakness he doesn't share, but he recognizes it as a form of power. Possession of beauty is possession of power. People will risk their lives for gold, which is a shiny lump of rock, while they would haggle over the price of potatoes. The best way to hold power is to hold what someone finds beautiful in your hand, so they know that at if they displease you, you can crush that. Informs Gardner of new chef's arrival.
