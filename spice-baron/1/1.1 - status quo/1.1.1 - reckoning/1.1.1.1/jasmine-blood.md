The sumptuous jasmine blossoms tried to swallow the scent of blood, but it was early evening and the blood was fresh. Rivulets ran down and through the petals of skin plied apart by the Baron's knife and dripped like dew into the seams between the cobblestones below the dais. The cobblestones rejected the man's screams and they scattered themselves across the courtyard and saturated the sultry summer air, seeping slowly into the walls of the manor and the stables where they'd ring for days. 

Word was he had squirrelled away some saffron to make a meal for the girl who now bore witness to his reckoning, one of the Baron's akanthes--his thorns as he called them. These trespasses meant the chef was twice-damned. The Reckoning had come down that very night. The guards had corralled everyone in the estate into a ring around the dais to watch. The girl was held by two guards with her face held inches away from the man who had dared to love her. She had tried to close her eyes, but one of the guards reached his hand over her forehead and peeled her eyelids open.

Gardner stared through the man, past him, to the wall of jasmine blossoms. It was not permitted to look away or close your eyes, so Gardner looked through and did not see the man growing faint from exsanguination, nor did he see the woman hald to face him and watch as we was slowly hollowed out into a shell of a man. Gardner had not known him well, which was a small mercy. It was harder to watch the ones you knew. 

Gardner looked past all of this, fixing his gaze on the star-burst blossoms in their folial firmament. Their scent was growing stronger now, almost overcoming the metal-sweet reek of the blood. Gardner breathed in deeply through his nose, willing the intoxicant to dull him to the world and drift him gently through the hours, til the man hung limp and husk-like in the frame and the Baron wiped his knives clean on the sleeves of his white shirt, til after the baron raised his blood-soaked arms to the sky and blessed the reckoning and said that justice had been served and harmony restored.
