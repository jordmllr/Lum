1.1.1.1 - Anticipitation for a Reckoning
We join gardner waiting in the courtyard. the dais is empty. the man is brought out and strapped into the oak frame, stretching the skin of his back tight. maybe Gardner is trying to fill himself with beauty to protect him from the coming violence. The jasmine, the breeze cooling his skin and dancing through the trees. He settles on the jasmine because he'll be able to stare at it and look like he's looking at the man. There's no looking away, unless you also want to be forced to watch the light leave the victim's eyes. This part ends when the man is brought out and the Baron takes the stage so to speak. 
- at this point we know who gardner is, we've been primed to meet the Baron, and we know that we're about to witness a live execution from which we are not allowed to look away. We know Gardner's defense is in natural beauty.

1.1.1.2 - The Baron's speech
The Baron walks out of the house. A woman trails behind him carrying a leather rolled up bundle. She places it on a long wooden table and leaves the dais. The Baron surveys the audience and then the man in the frame. He gives a speech where he talks about the misfortune of a reckoning. There are no secrets in my house. I am a generous man, and I cannot abide those who trespass on that generosity. I provide shelter and food and protection from the world out there. And all i ask in return is for your gratitude and loyalty. If we don't stick together, we fall apart. (maybe the knives get unrolled here.) We're no better than animals out in the cold. These are hard times. My grandfather knew these hard times would come, and he built this place as a haven to weather the coming storm. He saw glimpses out of time, and what he built has weathered the storm and it's my job to protect it. This gives us a little backstory and exposition as well as building the anticipation of the violence that's to come. At the end of the speech, he has the woman held down to 'bear witness' to what she has brought about. 
- This should give us a strong sense of the Baron and his double sidedness. His words contrasted with his revelrie in violence. Violence may have been the only thing he truly loved for its own sake. 

1.1.1.3 - The Reckoning
The Baron begins to make cuts. Gardner tries to lock in to the jasmine on the wall behind the man, to look past and through, to try to lose himself in beauty and memory. Here we talk about all the things he 'doesn't see' as he tries to dissociate. We vascillate between memories and scents. we get some flashes of backstory and descriptions of the grounds as Gardner tries to loose himself from the present. 

1.1.1.4 - Final part of this scene?
I'm not really sure what's supposed to happen here? What happens when the Reckoning ends? Is there a feast that brings home how the Baron and his guards rejoice in violence? Is the man's skin/body stretched on the frame so that he is laid bare for all to see what he was? It seems like it should be something that Gardner cannot ignore. This whole time, Gardner has been trying not to be seen or be present. He wants to leave, but can't yet. He's trying to escape but has to wait for something. At long last, he is able to get away and head towards the greenhouse.
