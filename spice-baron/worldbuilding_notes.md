# Worldbuilding Notes

## The Post-Collapse World

- Global apocalypse of unspecified character; the collapse validated August's predictions
- Approximately **100 years have passed** since the fall; the plantation is a generational institution now
- Spices, luxury goods, and agriculture are now power — not metaphorically but literally
- The world outside the plantation is mostly absent in Act 1, felt only through its pressure on the Baron's philosophy and the Chef's distant origin

## The Reckoning — The Baron's System of Public Punishment

- The Baron's term for public execution; the word implies debt settled, ledger balanced
- Attendance is mandatory; **looking away is forbidden**
- If a guard sees someone avert their eyes, they are brought forward to watch from closer range
- The cruelty is architectural: loved ones are positioned so the condemned can see them watching
- The Reckoning is not rage — it is administered with deliberate authority; that is what makes it a system rather than a tantrum
- Gardner has internalized these rules; he does not flinch, does not look away; endurance through perfect knowledge

## The Plantation as Civilization-in-Miniature

- A reproduction of old-world plantation society: a Baron, a private army, a captive workforce, a concubine class
- Not formally enslaved — but trapped; leaving means the swamp, the collapse, and whatever lies beyond, which is unknown and may be worse
- The absence of a legal category for their condition is part of the cruelty: there is no name for what they are, no outside system that would recognize it
- **The Baron**: rules through spectacle, possession, and the Reckoning
- **The guards**: small private army; kept loyal with alcohol, drugs, and access to the Girls; they are also trapped in their own way
- **The Girls**: the Baron's concubine; serve double duty as kitchen maids — this is how [UUID-B] (the first chef) came to know [UUID-A], and how Gardner has regular, unremarkable proximity to them; lent freely to the guards; several will act in Act 4
- The Baron does not worry about Gardner around the Girls because he has categorized Gardner as essentially a eunuch — beneath sexuality, beneath ambition, furniture; he cannot imagine Gardner or the Girls as agents; this contempt is the structural blindness that makes Act 4 possible
- **Grounds staff**: field workers, greenhouse laborers; Gardner moves among them; they are known to him by sight and labor, not individuated in the text; their anonymity is part of what the Baron's system does to people
- **House staff**: domestic workers inside the great house; similarly unnamed in the text

## Cobots

- Agricultural robots programmed and maintained by Gardner
- Gardner relates to them less as companions and more as **features of the environment** — like animals in a field, or weather patterns; present, purposeful, indifferent
- They are not organic, but they seem untouched by the world — the Baron's gaze does not land on them, they cannot be humiliated or owned in the way living things can
- Gardner may think or speak aloud near them the way one does near a dog or a body of water — not because they understand, but because their presence is safe
- Represent: pre-collapse technology (~100 years old), Gardner's expertise, and a quality of imperviousness he cannot access himself
- Gardner's envy of them: not that they are alive, but that they are **sealed against feeling**

## Yoruba — The Regional Power

- The dominant civilization of the post-collapse Southeast US; lays claim to most of the region
- **Twin capitals**:
  - **Ogun** — the military capital; named for the orisha of iron, war, and labor; the forge city
  - **Oshun** — the cultural capital; named for the orisha of rivers, love, and fertility; the living city
- Established quickly in the wake of the apocalypse and has been instrumental in restoring the region; its speed and coherence are part of its character — it did not wait for the old world to come back
- Kehinde likely moves between both capitals; her authority spans the military and political
- The outside world knows the Plantation exists and what it produces; the Baron maintains a curated face to traders — civilized, tasteful, dangerous in a polished way; the interior reality is not advertised
- Yoruba's interest in the Plantation is strategic: control of the spice supply is leverage in a world where luxury goods are power
- **Smaller settlements**: dotted around the region, outside Yoruba's full reach; harsher, less stable; some may trade with the Plantation on the Baron's terms; they have no power to check him

## Botanical Weapons — The Act 4 Mechanism

### Datura (*Datura stramonium* / Jimsonweed)
- Grows wild at the swamp margins of the Plantation; large white trumpet flowers, blooms at night, visually striking
- All parts contain scopolamine and atropine — causes genuine delirium, stupefaction, inability to coordinate or act
- **Used on the guards**: administered in their wine, poured by the Girls; onset 30–60 minutes orally, so the dinner proceeds normally before the guards begin to go strange
- The Girls may be complicit in the preparation — deepens their thread through the novel from the opening Reckoning
- Gardner cultivates or gathers it quietly; the cobots tend it without knowing what it is
- First planted in the narrative: a striking visual encounter with datura in the wild, possibly on the Act 2 foraging trip; Táiwò may note its violence

### Psilocybin Mushrooms
- Gathered on the Act 2 foraging trip into the woods outside the Plantation — Gardner's first time off it
- Táiwò identifies them, says something precise and knowing: they lay the mind bare to the beauty of the world
- They may trip together on that trip — an early intimacy, a cracking open of Gardner
- Gardner dries and keeps a portion, quietly, for months
- **Used on Gardner and the Baron** in Act 4: added to the wine or food at the final dinner
- Gardner doses himself knowingly and has set an intention; the Baron is dosed unknowingly
- Both enter an altered state, but Gardner is steering and the Baron is lost
- The Baron's feverish speech, his growing paranoia, his noticing something is wrong — all now have a double register: he is also tripping
- Gardner's speech about "the true beauty of the world" comes from this place — earned by the mushrooms and by everything that preceded them
