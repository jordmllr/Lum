# Act 1 — Narration Notes

## Gardner's Physical Presence on the Page

- Tall, lean, dark-skinned; has learned to make himself small — contracted posture, minimized presence; the body's long adaptation to a world where being noticed is dangerous
- With the plants he is different: his hands are full and certain, gentle, almost as if the plants were an extension of his own body
- This contrast — the small man and the sure hands — is how Gardner should be rendered in action; the prose should hold both at once

## POV and Distance

- Close third person, tethered to Gardner throughout Acts 1–3
- Gardner's interiority is observant, restrained, body-conscious — he notices work, materials, posture, risk
- His synaesthesia should inflect the prose subtly: sensory crossings, not clinical descriptions of the condition
- He should not sound academically reflective; when he speaks or thinks at length, it must be earned by pressure

## Tonal Register by Location

- **Courtyard**: Spare, taut, ceremonial. Syntax tightens. Violence lands clean.
- **Greenhouses**: Most expansive register. Heat, fragrance, condensation, the cobots' quiet motion. Sensuality and dread together.
- **Vanilla/Coffee section**: Slightly more open, more August's world than the Baron's — the prose can breathe a little more here before the Baron arrives and closes it down.

## The Synaesthesia

- Gardner's synaesthesia is not a superpower — it is an affliction as much as a gift
- Feeling powerfully and deeply is oppressive; he cannot turn it off
- The greenhouse is refuge partly because the sensory richness there is *chosen* — he is fluent in it — versus the courtyard where sensation is forced on him as violation
- The cobots represent sealed, feeling-free existence; Gardner's envy of them is the envy of exhaustion

## Imagery Anchors for Act 1

- **Orchid bloom**: fragile, fleshy, one-day life, the Baron's casual destruction of it
- **Cinnamon peel**: red-brown, curling, the smell almost covering the iron of the courtyard
- **Vanilla flower**: pale, greenish-yellow, intensely fragrant, briefly blooming — Gardner's nature mirrored
- **Cobots in the rows**: purposeful, silent, unreportable, immune

## The Blooming — Tonal and Craft Notes

- The passage is poetic, lyrical, and prolonged — not a quick violence but a sustained one
- Stays entirely within the botanical vocabulary: peeling, separating, plying, exposing, petal, striation, layer, husk, rind, membrane
- Suggestive rather than explicit — the horror lives in the metaphor holding, not in anatomical inventory
- Sample register (author's own): *"plying apart the petals of his skin to reveal the striated inner petals"* — this is the pitch to sustain
- The passage must not become boring; rhythm and progression matter; each beat should feel like a deliberate gardening act
- The Baron is reduced from owner to specimen, from subject to object — the language enacts that reversal
- The mirror is already placed before this begins; the Baron watches himself become a thing
- Gardner's emotional state during the blooming: not anger; precise, present, almost tender — the way he is with the plants; the mushrooms have opened him into something transcendental; he finds the gall at the center of the Baron and understands what he has always been looking at; the hatred releases not through forgiveness but through seeing clearly

## Gardner's Speech — Tonal Notes

- Not prepared; discovered in the moment; the mushrooms loosen something he has never been able to say
- Draws on August, the vanilla in bloom, Táiwò, the cobots tending the rows — everything he has loved and lost and been
- The first time in the novel Gardner speaks at length under no one's gaze but his own
- **The thesis**: his ability to see and feel beauty has always felt like weakness — a fragility, an affliction; but there is true power in beauty; not in its possession, not in the sway it holds over others, but in its ability to fill a person; without beauty, we are hollow
- This answers the Baron directly: the Baron spent his whole life exploiting others' capacity for beauty, convinced desire was weakness; Gardner is standing in the Baron's dining room, full of beauty, claiming it as strength
- The gall at the Baron's center and the hollow he claimed beauty creates in others are the same hollow — Gardner's speech answers both; beauty doesn't empty you; the inability to feel it does
- The speech is a coming-into-himself moment: he was defined by August, then the Baron, then Táiwò; now he claims himself and the Plantation as part of himself
- To find its full form in the writing; these notes are the shape, not the words

- The execution scene: short sentences, clean images, ceremonial pacing — not action-movie
- The greenhouse escape: expand the prose; let the scent and heat do work
- The Baron's speech: he should sound reasonable, even generous, which is the horror of it
