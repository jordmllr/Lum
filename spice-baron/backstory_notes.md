# Backstory Notes

## The World

- Near-future, post-global-apocalypse setting
- The collapse validated August's predictions: spices and luxury goods command enormous power
- The plantation exists in an altered South — the swamp geography persists, the social hierarchy has regressed and calcified

## The Plantation's Origins

- The plantation was commissioned by the Baron's **great-grandfather** — an oil-fortune heir — sometime in the **2030s**, before the collapse
- He foresaw the end and built a self-sufficient luxury-goods fortress in the Gulf swamp: gridshell greenhouses, fortified architecture, deep spice stock. He was rich and had travelled the world, collecting beautiful things and experiences and was driven by the horror of a world collapse destroying that beauty. This could actually be left ambiguous. Was he motivated by aesthetics or avarice?
- The collapse came; his prediction proved right; the plantation has now stood for roughly **100 years**
- August is a later generation — the great-grandfather's descendant — who inherited the estate and its philosophy but brought his own genuine feeling for the land
- August had real love for the plants; he passed this to Gardner
- His preference for Gardner over his own son (the Baron) is a wound that shapes everything that follows
- **The family name DeGall**: French-prefixed Gulf South aristocratic pretension; but *gall* is a botanical term — a growth a plant forms around a wound or parasite; the plant's own tissue, proliferating abnormally, hollow at the center, hardened around something it could never expel; the Baron is a DeGall in every sense; the name is the novel's hidden key, unlocked only in the blooming

## Gardner's Origin

- Half-feral when August found/took him in — no stable family, no prior world
- The plantation is the only home he has known; August was the only father-figure
- This makes his entrapment under the Baron doubly painful: the Baron has inherited August's place and corrupted it

## August's Death — The Shadow Question

- The Baron reportedly mixed August a nightly tincture toward the end of his life
- Gardner may have been quietly jealous of that intimacy at the time — the Baron's proximity to August in his final months
- Now that jealousy curdles into horror: was the tincture care, or was it the mechanism?
- This should remain unconfirmed in the text — a shadow, not a revelation

## The Baron's Inheritance

- The Baron did not build this world; it was built by his great-grandfather, passed down across generations, and arrived to him already a legend — he has only reoriented it toward possession and display
- Where August stewarded, the Baron inventories
- The plantation's beauty is real but now serves the Baron's philosophy: it exists to make others want what he has
