# Setting Notes

## The Plantation — Overview

- The plantation has no proper name in the text — it is simply *the Plantation*
- Family name: **DeGall** — French-prefixed, old Gulf South colonial pretension; background color only; gall is bitterness, and also the growth a living thing forms around a wound

- Located in a Gulf swamp region (Florida or Louisiana register): twisting live oaks, Spanish moss, wetland, heat, standing water
- Built by August as equal parts **fortress and plantation** — defensible, self-contained, a hedge against civilizational collapse
- Massive **gridshell greenhouses** built into the landscape; architectural feat, now also a cage
- The estate contains the plantation grounds, a great house (the Baron's domain), courtyards, and the greenhouse complex

## The Greenhouses

- The primary refuge for Gardner; where he is most himself
- Hot, wet, fragrant — condensation silvering the glass, leaves slick, bark dark with moisture
- Organized, numbered, named — the Baron's ownership expressed through taxonomic control
- Cobots tend the rows alongside Gardner; their quiet, purposeful movement is part of the texture of the space
- Most lush register in the prose; this is where sensuality and threat braid most naturally

## The Vanilla and Coffee Section

- A specific area within or adjacent to the greenhouse complex — **partially open-air or under an older, less fortified structure**
- Feels like something August built before the Baron hardened everything; less fortress, more original vision
- In Act 1, the vanilla is **in bloom** — fragrant, fleeting, the blossoms lasting only a day or two
- Vanilla is an orchid (Vanilla planifolia); its bloom is delicate, pale greenish-yellow, intensely fragrant
- This is where Gardner escapes after witnessing the chef's execution
- The Baron follows him here and delivers his speech on power and ownership — **he plucks an orchid bloom, crushes its fleshy petals, and drops it to the ground** — a casual, legible act of desecration

## The Tower

- An elevated structure on the Plantation — a water tower or pre-collapse industrial relic (oil derrick, utility tower); rusting, repurposed into nothing
- One of Gardner's private escape places; the Baron does not go there, does not think about it
- From the top, Gardner can see above the canopy — above the live oaks and Spanish moss and greenhouse glass — to the horizon; the only place on the Plantation where the world beyond it is visible
- Gardner bringing Táiwò here is the first voluntary act of trust he has made with another person since August died
- The lighthouse/lookout scene: Táiwò sees the horizon Gardner has always been forbidden to want; Gardner is the one who knows every root and bird call below — the inversion makes him briefly equal rather than below

## The Great House

- The Baron's domain; interior is overfull — collections pressed into every surface across four generations of DeGall accumulation; taxidermied things, inherited objects, proof of ownership stacked and displayed; the room itself makes the Baron's argument about having
- **The kitchen**: working space shared by Gardner and the Girls in their capacity as kitchen maids; unremarkable to the Baron, which is why it becomes the staging ground for Act 4
- **The dining room**: stage for the final dinner in Act 4; the Baron holds court in the middle of all his things; the excess should feel almost satirical against what Gardner is quietly doing with the wine

## The Courtyard

- Site of ritual violence and public spectacle
- Spare, dry, ceremonial — the prose should tighten here
- Where the chef's execution (Blood Eagle variant) takes place at the opening of Act 1
- Where Gardner is dragged again in Act 4
