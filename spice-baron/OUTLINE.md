# 1 - the new chef
## summary
We get our introduction to Gardner and the world of the Baron. During an execution of the chef for a minor offense, Gardner tries to focus on something else, disassociate. As soon as he's able, he escapes to his greenhouse, but he is interrupted by the Baron, remininding him that there is nowhere to hide, that he belongs to the Baron.
## 1.1 - Status Quo
### 1.1.1 - Gardner struggles to not watch the Reckoning
#### 1.1.1.1 - Anticipitation for a Reckoning
We join gardner waiting in the courtyard. the dais is empty. the man is brought out and strapped into the oak frame, stretching the skin of his back tight. maybe Gardner is trying to fill himself with beauty to protect him from the coming violence. The jasmine, the breeze cooling his skin and dancing through the trees. He settles on the jasmine because he'll be able to stare at it and look like he's looking at the man. There's no looking away, unless you also want to be forced to watch the light leave the victim's eyes. This part ends when the man is brought out and the Baron takes the stage so to speak. 
- at this point we know who gardner is, we've been primed to meet the Baron, and we know that we're about to witness a live execution from which we are not allowed to look away. We know Gardner's defense is in natural beauty.
#### 1.1.1.2 - The Baron's speech
The Baron walks out of the house. A woman trails behind him carrying a leather rolled up bundle. She places it on a long wooden table and leaves the dais. The Baron surveys the audience and then the man in the frame. He gives a speech where he talks about the misfortune of a reckoning. There are no secrets in my house. I am a generous man, and I cannot abide those who trespass on that generosity. I provide shelter and food and protection from the world out there. And all i ask in return is for your gratitude and loyalty. If we don't stick together, we fall apart. (maybe the knives get unrolled here.) We're no better than animals out in the cold. These are hard times. My grandfather knew these hard times would come, and he built this place as a haven to weather the coming storm. He saw glimpses out of time, and what he built has weathered the storm and it's my job to protect it. This gives us a little backstory and exposition as well as building the anticipation of the violence that's to come. At the end of the speech, he has the woman held down to 'bear witness' to what she has brought about. 
- This should give us a strong sense of the Baron and his double sidedness. His words contrasted with his revelrie in violence. Violence may have been the only thing he truly loved for its own sake. 
#### 1.1.1.3 - The Reckoning
The Baron begins to make cuts. Gardner tries to lock in to the jasmine on the wall behind the man, to look past and through, to try to lose himself in beauty and memory. Here we talk about all the things he 'doesn't see' as he tries to dissociate. We vascillate between memories and scents. we get some flashes of backstory and descriptions of the grounds as Gardner tries to loose himself from the present. 
#### 1.1.1.4 - Final part of this scene?
I'm not really sure what's supposed to happen here? What happens when the Reckoning ends? Is there a feast that brings home how the Baron and his guards rejoice in violence? Is the man's skin/body stretched on the frame so that he is laid bare for all to see what he was? It seems like it should be something that Gardner cannot ignore. This whole time, Gardner has been trying not to be seen or be present. He wants to leave, but can't yet. He's trying to escape but has to wait for something. At long last, he is able to get away and head towards the greenhouse.
### 1.1.2 - Gardner escapes to Vanilla, but Baron follows
#### summary
we've just come off of the first scene, where the Baron has grotesquely executed someone. So far, it is unclear what Gardner's relationship to the Baron is. This scene is going to establish their closeness. The Baron pays special attention to Gardner. 
#### 1.1.2.1 - Gardner makes his way to the greenhouse
we get some more sense of the setting and layout of the plantation. We also get some of Gardners internal thoughts as he tries to leave behind the reckoning.
#### 1.1.2.2 - Gardner gathers the pollination supplies and begins to lose himself in his work
one of the tenders(keepers?) has drifted in it's pollination skill. Gardner teaches it the motion. He remembers being taught by the Baron's father. The beauty of the year unfolding in its cycles. Just as he finishes, the machine shoots down the track and the Baron enters.
#### 1.1.2.3 - Baron monologues - beauty's power
Baron comes into the greenhouse and asks him about the reckoning. Gardner is quiet and tries to be small and acquiesce. The Baron taunts him for being such a fragile flower. All this time he is caressing the orchids talking about the irrational power of beauty. How even a starving man will seek it out, even if it is a waste of time or energy. 
#### 1.1.2.4 - Baron crushes the vanilla orchid - beauty is power over others
Brings home his philosophy that beauty is a weakness he doesn't share, but he recognizes it as a form of power. Possession of beauty is possession of power. People will risk their lives for gold, which is a shiny lump of rock, while they would haggle over the price of potatoes. The best way to hold power is to hold what someone finds beautiful in your hand, so they know that at if they displease you, you can crush that. Informs Gardner of new chef's arrival. When will this shipment of vanilla be ready and what's the yield? Good Gardner, you and I make a great team.
### 1.1.3 - Gardner wishes to be a machine, but won't betray beauty
#### 1.1.3.1 - bury the orchid
Gardner displays a care and loss of the flower. He can give it dignity that the chef didn't have. He thought of the new chef. Wondered how long it would be before he met the same fate or worse.
#### 1.1.3.2 - the enviable ignorance of machines
Gardner watches the tenders(keepers?) move back and forth on their tracks. They have no concept of beauty or loss or violence. They only now what the next movement is. Gardner wonders whether he would trade his life for theirs.
#### 1.1.3.3 - we cannot deny our natures
Gardner moves up in place of one of the machines and tries his best to only do the work. But he ends up entranced and just staring at the orchid. He comes up and realizes that it's been 20 minutes. We cannot deny our natures and his was to be cursed by the affliction of beauty.
#### 1.1.3.4 - leaves the greenhouse
We get a tour through some of the greenhouses as he makes his way to where he lives. He ponders the Baron's relationship with beauty. Seemingly immune except for the power it brings. Like a hollow, brittle shell. If you cracked him open, what would you find inside?
### 1.1.4 - Gardner goes to his home feeling hopeless
#### 1.1.4.1 - Gardner gets back to his perch in the watchtower
Gardner gets back to his little hovel. He goes about winding down. He places things just so, so that when he lays down, it will be beautiful. He takes care of his houseplants. Maybe bonsai? He is maybe doing some breeding experiments.
#### 1.1.4.2 - Tries to remember August and his early days and happy times
He tries to remember the good times. Goes through some of his treasured things. A plant identification book, some old photographs. Maybe he tears the Baron out of them.
#### 1.1.4.3 - Gardner has a nightmare
He falls asleep but a nightmare wakes him up. He climbs out up onto the roof, letting the night air and his sweat cool him. 
#### 1.1.4.4 - He looks out to sea, and sees the inescapable cycles yawning out before him
Staring out at the sea, feeling alone and lost, with the void yawning out before him. He thinks about the days and months to come. The cycle of beauty and nature and violence and power repeating itself over and over again. He thinks about the new chef coming. It'll be cinnamon season then. And goes to bed. 
## 1.2 - The New Chef Arrives
### summary
We have already established The Baron and Gardner at this point. We have a strong picture of Gardner's life. Scene 2 is where we are going to interrupt the status quo by introducing Taiwo.
### 1.2.1 - cinnamon peeling, Gardner daydreams about romance
i want to somehow work in Michael Ondantjee's poem the cinnamon peeler. It may not be here, but it is a beautiful poem about belonging, possessing, and being marked by love. it has passion, violence and romance that fit well in this story.

The robots dance in circles and cut the bark open with a laser. the smell of roast cinnamon is thick in the air. He tries to give himself to it and be lost in it. Gardner is stacking the collars beside the trees. It's hot and Gardner has his shirt off. He hears the door open, and he cringes. The Baron's the only one who visits here. Baron calls and he turns around. The Baron is accompanied by another man. Baron introduces him to the new chef. Gardner all of a sudden feels naked and puts on his shirt. Taiwo says not to bother on his account. It's so hot, he's tempted to do the same.   
### 1.2.2 - the new chef is surprisingly bold with the Baron
Baron says that this chef is the jewel of Yoruba. A culinary genius. The envy of the world. The new chef introduces himself as Taiwo. The new chef has a confidence and exuberance that's unusual on the estate. Taiwo addresses Gardner directly, asking him questions about the plantation, goes to one of the collars and scrapes some of the dust and tastes it (Gardner shocked that the Baron allows this), asking if this or that is grown here. The Baron says everything is grown here. Gardner corrects the Baron and instantly regrets it, but Taiwo is able to diffuse the situation by suggesting they will have to steal some from Yoruba. It needs a real gardner to tend it. Yoruban horticulture is decades behind this place. The Baron feels pride in his possession of Gardner again. Baron says that's why they are there actually. They need to collect ingredients for a feast that's coming up in a week's days time. The baron leaves Gardner to get Taiwo anything he needs. It's a very important dinner, and a chance for Taiwo to show off his talents. 
### 1.2.3 - a tour and collecting ingredients for dinner
Gardner asks Taiwo what he needs. Taiwo says he isn't sure yet. He needs to see the whole garden, but he's particular curious about fruits and chile peppers. They wander through the various chambers chatting about the operations. Is it just you? Taiwo asks. Just me, and the tenders, Gardner said. They watch one of the robots shuttle down a track. 'Must get lonely'. Gardner doesn't know how to respond. 'Yes, let's continue'. They travel some more. Finally, they get to a fruit bearing plant (maybe a cactus? maybe a fig?). Taiwo doesn't recognize it, asks if it is edible. Gardner says yes. Taiwo asks what it tastes like. Gardner says he doesn't know, they are not supposed to eat them. Taiwo plucks one and cuts a piece out with his knife and gives a piece to Gardner. Taiwo thinks for a bit, then pulls salt out of his little hip pouch. hands it again. Gardner blown away by the flavor not of salt but like the fruit but more intense. Then sugar. then vinegar. 

We aren't supposed to eat these. Nonsense! Taiwo says. How are we (gardner thrills at the word we) supposed to create the best cuisine the world has ever seen if we can't taste the world! I was born a twin, when twins are born in my culture, they are given the names Taiwo and Kehinde. Kehinde, my sister, is the older and she sent me out into the world to taste it and tell her if it is good. I guess I never stopped. The world is such a delicious place.

Maybe worth adding in here that Gardner's synaesthesia is intense and Taiwo sees it. He asks what's wrong and Gardner tries to describe it. Taiwo knows too, because he is also synaesthetic. He used to struggle as a child, but then he learned that he could learn to bring it all together. It was a strength, a gift, not a weakness or a curse.

The rest of the tour becomes almost indulgent. Gardner becomes so curious about Taiwo. He takes him to all of the plants he ahs been curious about that he has never tasted. It is as if his life had been lived in only a single color and now Taiwo and tilted the light through a prism and scattered it into a million bands of color distinct and bleeding into each other. Each time, they taste with salt, then sweet, then sour.
Gardner crashes into his bed again and even though he closes his eyes, he can still see and hear and taste all those colors.

probably should add him seeing something that still reminds him of where he is. Maybe guards taking a woman into the barracks, or doing some other such cruelty. He worries that the new chef may find himself on the frame one day.
### 1.2.4 - the first meal impresses the Baron and intrigues Gardner
a taste of what Taiwo's cooking prowess is like. The akantha help him serve a beautiful dinner. The Baron is impressed. Taiwo asks Gardner his opinion as the one who grew the things. This begins Taiwo's interruption of traditions. Gardner feels exposed and seen, not invisible, but likes Taiwo's attention. He leaves feeling something like hope, but hope for what? he's not sure.

Taiwo wants to run the plantation and develop it into a culinary research station. To explore the world, collecting and tasting it, and sharing the delicious anchor of life (eating a meal) with others. He stands in contrast to the Baron. Baron wants to keep everything fror himself out of greed and pride. Taiwo wants to feed the world. He mentions the seedbank up in Svalbard. Maybe he and Gardner will go there one day and spread the seeds around the world.

Gardner doesn't see Taiwo for a few days as he plans for a feast. Taiwo takes Gardner on a foraging excursion. Gardner asks him why he's there. Taiwo explains that this is the best place in the world for a chef. He talks about the sorry state of the world and how this place could bring so much joy to the world. It's through the alchemy of cooking that these plants become transcendental. Gardner loves them for what they are, Taiwo loves them for what they could be.
