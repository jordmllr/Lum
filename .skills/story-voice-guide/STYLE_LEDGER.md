# Style Ledger

## Story context
- Working title: Spice Baron
- Genre: Southern gothic science fiction with romantic tragedy and revenge horror
- POV: Close third person, anchored tightly to Gardner for the main body; epilogue may widen or switch for contrast
- Tense: Past tense
- Audience: Adult readers comfortable with lyrical violence, sensual detail, and oppressive power dynamics
- Core aesthetic: Decadent plantation rot after the end of the world; botanical abundance pressed against cruelty, ownership, hunger, and beauty

## Current voice decisions
- Narrative distance: Intimate and body-aware. Stay near Gardner's perceptions, especially fear, hunger, awe, and shame. Avoid omniscient moral framing; let judgment arise from what he notices and endures.
- Sentence rhythm: Mostly controlled and elegant, with occasional long, winding sentences when the prose enters sensory overwhelm, desire, or hallucination. Violence should sharpen into cleaner, harder beats.
- Diction level: Literary but not ornamental for its own sake. Favor concrete nouns, plant terms, culinary vocabulary, ritual language, and the language of ownership, husbandry, and preservation.
- Imagery density: Rich and sensuous in greenhouse, kitchen, and Yoruba scenes. Moderate elsewhere. Every lush passage should still carry threat, labor, appetite, or grief.
- Humor level: Very low. No quips. Any wit should come as cold irony, social menace, or the Baron's theatrical vanity.
- Dialogue texture: Measured, charged, and slightly formal. The Baron speaks as if possession grants philosophy. Gardner is more spare and observant. The Chef should sound precise, cultivated, and sensuous without becoming flowery.

## Do
- Treat beauty as dangerous, not comforting. Fragrance, color, heat, and texture should often arrive with control, rot, blood, debt, or desire attached.
- Use botanical and culinary imagery as the story's primary symbolic systems. Plants, grafting, blooming, peeling, ripening, distilling, preserving, and spoilage should structure description.
- Keep Gardner's class position visible in the prose. He notices labor, tools, scars, waste, posture, and the cost of luxury.
- Let the world feel post-apocalyptic through scarcity and salvage, not exposition dumps. Mention broken systems only where they touch daily ritual, trade, greenhouse infrastructure, or social order.
- Make Yoruba feel genuinely expansive rather than merely nicer. The contrast should be civic, botanical, architectural, and moral: ordered abundance instead of private hoarding.
- Write violence with clarity and ceremony. The horror should be legible and deliberate, not splashy.
- Use recurring contrasts: hothouse versus courtyard, scent versus blood, cultivation versus ownership, appetite versus tenderness, preservation versus decay.

## Don't
- Do not write generic dystopian grit. This world is fragrant, cultivated, stratified, and theatrical.
- Do not let the prose become purple by stacking too many modifiers or metaphors in the same sentence.
- Do not explain themes directly when image and scene can carry them.
- Do not give the Baron snappy villain banter. His menace works best when he sounds calm, proprietary, and convinced of his own refinement.
- Do not flatten the Chef into a moral lesson or exotic ideal. He should feel skilled, worldly, desirous, and strategically alive.
- Do not let romance turn soft-focus. Intimacy should remain shaped by danger, hunger, and the possibility of loss.
- Do not describe brutality in a detached thriller register. It should feel ceremonial, intimate, and appallingly observed.
